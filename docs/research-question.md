
Research question
Origin

The original intuition behind the project was that the repeated appearance of
decimal digits might create a measurable form of cycle maturity.

That intuition can be expressed in two very different ways.

Delayed-digit hypothesis

After a digit has remained absent for several draws, does its probability of
appearing on the next draw increase?

The analyses performed on 2025 data did not find a stable or monotonic increase
after controlling for the natural probability of each digit and position.

The apparent increase observed in some small samples did not replicate.

Coverage-completion hypothesis

Given the set of digits still missing from the current cycle, what is the
probability that the next draw, or one of the next few draws, completes the
coverage?

This question is mathematically well-defined.

The probability does not arise because a missing digit is due. It arises
because the coverage process is already close to its absorbing state.

Central distinction

The project therefore adopts the following principle:

The individual digit does not accumulate probability; the coverage
accumulates completeness.

A cycle missing only {3} is objectively closer to completion than a fresh
cycle missing all ten digits.

A cycle missing {9} is less mature than a cycle missing {3}, despite both
having one digit missing, because digit 9 occurs in fewer numbers from 1 to
90.

Research objectives

The project aims to:

calculate exact completion probabilities;
model transitions between coverage states;
estimate the exact expected residual duration;
verify calibration on historical data;
replay historical draws without future leakage;
publish forecasts before future draws;
preserve all forecasts immutably for cumulative scoring.
Non-objectives

The project does not aim to:

identify winning numbers;
create betting combinations;
prove that delayed numbers are due;
optimize gambling expenditure;
claim a monetary edge.
